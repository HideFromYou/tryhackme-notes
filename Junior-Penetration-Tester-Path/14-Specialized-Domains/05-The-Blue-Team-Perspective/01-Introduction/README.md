# 01 - Introduction

## Overview

This room introduces the Blue Team perspective and explains how defensive security operations work from the inside.

As a penetration tester, understanding how defenders detect, investigate, and respond to attacks makes offensive testing more realistic and useful.

The room focuses on:

    SOC Operations
    SIEM
    Log Analysis
    Incident Response
    Threat Intelligence
    MITRE ATT&CK
    Investigation

---

## Why Blue Team Knowledge Matters

A penetration tester who understands defensive operations can identify not only vulnerabilities, but also the detection gaps surrounding them.

A useful pentesting mindset is:

    Attack
      ↓
    Detection
      ↓
    Investigation
      ↓
    Response

Understanding this process helps a pentester understand what defenders would actually see during an engagement.

## Learning Objectives

By completing the room, you will understand:

- How a SOC is structured.
- How SOC analysts triage alerts.
- How SIEM platforms centralise security data.
- How to search and investigate logs.
- How common attack patterns appear in event data.
- How incident response is structured.
- How threat intelligence and MITRE ATT&CK support investigations.
- How multiple findings can be correlated into an attack chain.

---

## Pentester Perspective

The goal is not to become a defender instead of a pentester.

The goal is to understand both sides.

    Red Team
       ↓
    Attack Technique
       ↓
    Blue Team
       ↓
    Detection / Investigation
       ↓
    Security Improvement

This perspective becomes especially valuable when writing professional penetration-testing reports.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Blue Team knowledge improves offensive security skills.
- A vulnerability is only part of a real-world attack.
- Detection, investigation, and response determine how an organisation reacts.
- SIEM, logs, incident response, and threat intelligence are core defensive capabilities.
- Understanding defensive visibility helps pentesters assess the effectiveness of security controls.
