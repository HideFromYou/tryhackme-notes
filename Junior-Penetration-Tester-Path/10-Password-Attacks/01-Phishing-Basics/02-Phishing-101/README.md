# Phishing 101

## Overview

**Phishing** is a form of cyber attack that uses **social engineering** to manipulate victims into revealing sensitive information or executing malicious content. Instead of exploiting technical vulnerabilities, phishing exploits **human psychology**, convincing users to trust messages that appear to come from legitimate sources.

Attackers commonly deliver phishing attacks through emails, SMS messages, voice calls, or fake websites. Their objectives may include stealing credentials, obtaining financial information, gaining unauthorized access, or installing malware on a victim's device.

## Main Content

### What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyber attack that relies on **social engineering** rather than technical exploitation.

Attackers impersonate trusted individuals or organizations to persuade victims to:

- Reveal sensitive information
- Submit login credentials
- Open malicious attachments
- Execute malware
- Visit fraudulent websites

The success of phishing depends on manipulating human behavior rather than exploiting software vulnerabilities.

---

### Common Delivery Methods

Phishing attacks are commonly delivered through:

- Email
- SMS (**Smishing**)
- Voice calls (**Vishing**)
- Fake websites

Each method attempts to convince victims that the communication is legitimate.

---

### Types of Phishing

#### Phishing

Traditional phishing uses the same generic message against a large number of victims.

Characteristics include:

- Mass distribution
- Generic messages
- Common themes such as invoices or account alerts
- Focus on stealing credentials or financial information

---

#### Spear Phishing

Spear phishing targets a **specific individual**.

Messages are customized to increase credibility and usually attempt to persuade the victim to:

- Click malicious links
- Open infected attachments
- Submit credentials
- Execute attacker-controlled files

---

#### Whaling

Whaling is a specialized form of spear phishing aimed at **senior executives** such as:

- CEOs
- CFOs
- Executive management

These attacks seek access to high-value information or the ability to authorize financial transactions.

---

### Phishing in Penetration Testing

Phishing is frequently included in penetration tests to evaluate an organization's resistance to social engineering.

Ethical hackers simulate realistic phishing campaigns by:

- Sending controlled phishing emails
- Targeting selected employee groups
- Measuring open rates
- Measuring click-through rates
- Evaluating employee awareness

The objective is to identify human weaknesses without causing harm and provide recommendations to improve organizational security.

## Key Takeaways

- Phishing is a social engineering attack that targets people rather than technical vulnerabilities.
- Common delivery methods include email, SMS (smishing), voice calls (vishing), and fake websites.
- Traditional phishing targets many users, spear phishing targets specific individuals, and whaling targets senior executives.
- Phishing assessments help organizations identify weaknesses in employee awareness.
- Simulated phishing campaigns are an important component of modern penetration testing.
